TITLE:
Evaluating Size Generalization and Guidance Overhead in Learned Combinational Circuit Repair

AUTHORS:
Anonymous Authors (Under Double-Blind Review)

PRIMARY SUBJECT AREA:
Applications to Hardware, Software, and System Verification / Automated Reasoning / Neuro-Symbolic Synthesis

SECONDARY SUBJECT AREA:
Empirical Evaluations and Benchmark Methodologies

KEYWORDS:
circuit repair, size generalization, guidance overhead, formal verification, Boolean satisfiability (SAT), counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS), neuro-symbolic systems, empirical evaluation

TL;DR:
A controlled 9,000-trial benchmark showing that lightweight decision forests trained on small circuits match SAT on small scale but lag modern factored CNF (Glucose SAT) on larger circuits under tight deadlines (<= 1.0s) due to guidance overhead and cross-scale ranking imprecision, converging at 10.0s.

ABSTRACT (PLAIN TEXT FOR OPENREVIEW):
Machine learning guidance is frequently proposed to accelerate combinatorial search by prioritizing candidate modifications. In automated program and circuit synthesis, exhaustive ground-truth generation is computationally tractable only on small instances, making out-of-distribution size generalization from small to larger designs a central open challenge. In this work, we present a controlled empirical evaluation examining whether lightweight decision forests trained on small combinational circuits (n in {4, 5} primary inputs, G in {10, 14} logic gates) can generalize cross-scale to guide single-edit repair on larger combinational circuits (n <= 16, G <= 200) under strict wall-clock deadlines (0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s).

Evaluating a frozen protocol of 9,000 trials across 300 synthetic 2-LUT circuits and ten distinct repair methods, our results demonstrate that a direct, factored Boolean CNF encoding solved via Glucose 4.2.1 in a Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS) loop substantially outperforms the learned approach on larger circuits under tight operational deadlines (<= 1.0s). At a 0.1-second deadline on the 250 larger circuits, direct CNF achieves an 81.6% repair rate (204/250) compared to 21.6% (54/250) for the learned ranker. Performance converges at a 10.0-second budget (81.6% vs. 80.8%, delta = -0.8 percentage points, 95% CI [-2.0, 0.0]), where the learned ranker repairs 202 of the 204 solvable instances (99.0%).

Crucially, size-stratified analysis reveals that on small circuits close to the training distribution (n=6, G=18), the learned ranker matches direct CNF across all deadlines (delta = 0.0 at 0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s). However, as circuit scale increases (G=50 to 100 to 200), performance degrades under tight deadlines due to two compounding factors: (1) upfront guidance overhead (averaging 143.1 ms: 50.3 ms feature extraction, 90.4 ms forest inference, and 2.5 ms sorting, compared to 16.7 ms for complete CNF solving and verification), and (2) ranking imprecision under cross-size distribution shift (requiring an average of 336.6 candidate checks at ~0.63 ms per check, totaling 211.9 ms of sequential verification). An ablation study confirms that target-conditioned need features improve repair rate at 1.0s by +15.6 percentage points over unconditioned features, but this reflects access to specification values rather than an intrinsic advantage of backward message propagation. All accepted repairs were verified with zero incorrect acceptances. We discuss the implications of these empirical measurements for neuro-symbolic system design and benchmark transparency.
