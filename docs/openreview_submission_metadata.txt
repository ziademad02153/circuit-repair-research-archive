TITLE:
Guidance Overhead Across Circuit Scale in Learned Combinational Circuit Repair

AUTHORS:
Anonymous Author (Under Double-Blind Review)

PRIMARY SUBJECT AREA:
Applications to Hardware, Software, and System Verification / Automated Reasoning / Neuro-Symbolic Synthesis

SECONDARY SUBJECT AREA:
Empirical Evaluations and Benchmark Methodologies

KEYWORDS:
circuit repair, guidance overhead, circuit scale, formal verification, Boolean satisfiability (SAT), counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS), neuro-symbolic systems, empirical evaluation

TL;DR:
A controlled 9,000-trial benchmark showing that lightweight decision forests trained on compact circuits match SAT on small scale and intermediate circuits (n <= 12) at 1.0s, but lag modern factored CNF (Glucose SAT) under sub-second deadlines due to candidate-scaling guidance overhead (~8 us/cand), while outperforming non-factored heuristics at 1.0s and reaching parity with Z3 SMT at 10.0s.

ABSTRACT (PLAIN TEXT FOR OPENREVIEW):
Machine learning guidance is frequently proposed to accelerate combinatorial search by prioritizing candidate modifications. In this work, we present a controlled empirical evaluation examining whether lightweight decision forests trained on compact combinational circuits (n in {4, 5} primary inputs, G in {10, 14} logic gates) can effectively guide single-edit repair on larger combinational circuits (n <= 16, G <= 200) under strict wall-clock deadlines (0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s). Evaluating a frozen protocol of 9,000 trials across 300 synthetic 2-LUT circuits and ten repair methods, our results demonstrate that a direct, factored Boolean CNF encoding solved via Glucose 4.2.1 in a CEGIS loop substantially outperforms the learned approach under tight deadlines (<= 1.0s). At 0.1s on 250 larger circuits, direct CNF achieves an 81.6% repair rate (204/250) compared to 20.8% for the primary validated learned ranker, because upfront guidance overhead (107.7-428.6 ms for G >= 100, scaling near-linearly at 7.5-8.8 us per candidate) exceeds the entire 100 ms budget before search can begin. At 1.0s, direct CNF maintains an advantage overall (81.6% vs. 74.4%, delta = -7.2 percentage points, 95% CI [-10.4, -4.4]), although the learned ranker matches CNF on configurations with n <= 12. The 18-instance deficit at 1.0s is confined to n=16 circuits, where tail ranking interacts with 2^16-input verification throughput; extending the deadline to 10.0s resolves 15 of these 18 instances. At 10.0s, performance converges across five of six scale strata (81.6% vs. 80.4%, leaving a 3-instance gap on n=16, G=200). In total at 10.0s, the learned ranker repairs 201 of the 204 solvable larger instances (98.5%). Furthermore, learned ranking outperforms non-factored search heuristics (+8.4 pp) and unspecialized SMT (+17.6 pp) at 1.0s, while reaching parity with SMT at 10.0s (201 vs. 200). These results highlight the necessity of comparing learned guidance against factored symbolic baselines and accounting for complete proposal latencies across problem scales.
