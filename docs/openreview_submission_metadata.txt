TITLE:
Guidance Overhead Across Circuit Scale in Learned Combinational Circuit Repair

AUTHORS:
Anonymous Author (Under Double-Blind Review)

PRIMARY SUBJECT AREA:
Applications to Hardware, Software, and System Verification / Automated Reasoning / Neuro-Symbolic Synthesis

SECONDARY SUBJECT AREA:
Empirical Evaluations and Benchmark Methodologies

KEYWORDS:
circuit repair, guidance overhead, circuit scale, formal verification, Boolean satisfiability (SAT), counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS), neuro-symbolic systems, empirical evaluation

TL;DR:
A controlled 9,000-trial benchmark showing that lightweight decision forests trained on compact circuits match SAT on small scale and intermediate circuits (n <= 12) at 1.0s, but lag modern factored CNF (Glucose SAT) under sub-second deadlines due to candidate space expansion driving near-linear guidance overhead (~8 us/cand), while outperforming all non-SAT heuristics at >= 1.0s.

ABSTRACT (PLAIN TEXT FOR OPENREVIEW):
Machine learning guidance is frequently proposed to accelerate combinatorial search by prioritizing candidate modifications. In automated program and circuit synthesis, training models on compact instances is a common experimental paradigm to bound data generation costs. However, whether lightweight learned rankers can maintain an operational advantage over modern Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) solvers as circuit scale increases remains an open empirical question. In this work, we present a controlled empirical evaluation examining whether lightweight decision forests trained on compact combinational circuits (n in {4, 5} primary inputs, G in {10, 14} logic gates) can effectively guide single-edit repair on larger combinational circuits (n <= 16, G <= 200) under strict wall-clock deadlines (0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s).

Evaluating a frozen protocol of 9,000 trials across 300 synthetic 2-LUT circuits and ten distinct repair methods, our results demonstrate that a direct, factored Boolean CNF encoding solved via Glucose 4.2.1 in a Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS) loop substantially outperforms the learned approach on larger circuits under sub-second deadlines (0.1s). At a 0.1-second deadline on the 250 larger circuits, direct CNF achieves an 81.6% repair rate (204/250) compared to 20.8% (52/250) for the primary validated learned ranker (forest_full_selected) and 21.6% (54/250) for the frozen pilot model (learned_frozen). At a 1.0-second deadline, direct CNF maintains an advantage overall (81.6% vs. 74.4%, delta = -7.2 percentage points, 95% CI [-10.4, -4.4]). Performance converges across five of the six scale strata at 10.0s, leaving a minor 3-instance gap on the largest configuration (n=16, G=200: 40 vs. 43, 80.0% vs. 86.0%), where the learned ranker repairs 201 of the 204 solvable instances (98.5%).

A post-hoc exploratory stratification reveals that performance is strongly scale-dependent. In this Python/NumPy implementation, guidance overhead scales near-linearly with the candidate edit count at approximately 7.5 to 8.8 us per candidate across all circuit sizes. On small circuits (n=6, G=18), overhead is negligible (5.6 ms), and the learned ranker matches direct CNF across all deadlines (delta = 0.0 at 0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s). On circuits with G >= 100, candidate space expansion causes guidance overhead to grow to 107.7-428.6 ms, exceeding the entire 0.1-second budget before candidate verification begins. At a 1.0-second deadline, the learned ranker matches CNF completely on all larger configurations with n <= 12 (delta = 0.0). The remaining 18-instance deficit at 1.0s is confined strictly to n=16, where tail-instance ranking degradation interacts with slower verification over 2^16 inputs (0.50-0.91 ms per check); 15 of these 18 deficit instances are successfully repaired when the deadline is extended to 10.0s. Furthermore, at budgets >= 1.0s, learned guidance decisively outperforms all non-SAT heuristic and SMT baselines. An ablation confirms that target-conditioned need features improve repair rates at 1.0s by +15.6 percentage points over unconditioned features, reflecting access to output specification values. We discuss the implications of guidance overhead for combinatorial synthesis across problem scales.
