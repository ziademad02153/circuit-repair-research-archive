TITLE:
Guidance Overhead Across Circuit Scale in Learned Combinational Circuit Repair

AUTHORS:
Anonymous Authors (Under Double-Blind Review)

PRIMARY SUBJECT AREA:
Applications to Hardware, Software, and System Verification / Automated Reasoning / Neuro-Symbolic Synthesis

SECONDARY SUBJECT AREA:
Empirical Evaluations and Benchmark Methodologies

KEYWORDS:
circuit repair, guidance overhead, circuit scale, formal verification, Boolean satisfiability (SAT), counterexample-guided inductive synthesis (CEGIS), neuro-symbolic systems, empirical evaluation

TL;DR:
A controlled 9,000-trial benchmark showing that lightweight decision forests trained on compact circuits match SAT on small scale but lag modern factored CNF (Glucose SAT) on larger circuits under sub-second deadlines due to candidate space expansion driving guidance overhead, with performance converging at longer deadlines.

ABSTRACT (PLAIN TEXT FOR OPENREVIEW):
Machine learning guidance is frequently proposed to accelerate combinatorial search by prioritizing candidate modifications. In automated program and circuit synthesis, training models on compact instances is a common experimental paradigm to bound data generation costs. However, whether lightweight learned rankers can maintain an operational advantage over modern Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) solvers as circuit scale increases remains an open empirical question. In this work, we present a controlled empirical evaluation examining whether lightweight decision forests trained on compact combinational circuits (n in {4, 5} primary inputs, G in {10, 14} logic gates) can effectively guide single-edit repair on larger combinational circuits (n <= 16, G <= 200) under strict wall-clock deadlines (0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s).

Evaluating a frozen protocol of 9,000 trials across 300 synthetic 2-LUT circuits and ten distinct repair methods, our results demonstrate that a direct, factored Boolean CNF encoding solved via Glucose 4.2.1 in a Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS) loop substantially outperforms the learned approach on larger circuits under sub-second deadlines (0.1s). At a 0.1-second deadline on the 250 larger circuits, direct CNF achieves an 81.6% repair rate (204/250) compared to 20.8% (52/250) for the primary validated learned ranker (forest_full_selected) and 21.6% (54/250) for the frozen pilot model (learned_frozen). At a 1.0-second deadline, direct CNF maintains an advantage overall (81.6% vs. 74.4%, delta = -7.2 percentage points, 95% CI [-10.4, -4.4]), but performance converges at 10.0s (81.6% vs. 80.4%, delta = -1.2 percentage points, 95% CI [-2.8, 0.0]), where the learned ranker repairs 201 of the 204 solvable instances (98.5%).

A post-hoc exploratory stratification across circuit scale configurations reveals that the performance deficit is strongly scale-dependent. On small circuits (n=6, G=18), the learned ranker matches direct CNF across all deadlines (delta = 0.0 at 0.1s, 1.0s, and 10.0s). At G >= 100, candidate space expansion causes upfront guidance overhead (feature extraction, forest inference, and sorting) to grow from 5.6 ms on small circuits up to 107.7-428.6 ms on large circuits. Because this overhead exceeds the entire 0.1-second deadline, learned search times out before candidate verification can proceed. At 1.0s, the learned ranker matches CNF completely on all larger configurations with n <= 12, with the remaining deficit confined strictly to n=16 where large input dimensions (2^16 vectors) increase simulation latency. An ablation confirms that target-conditioned need features improve repair rates at 1.0s by +15.6 percentage points over unconditioned features, but this reflects access to specification values rather than an intrinsic advantage of backward message propagation. All accepted repairs were verified with zero incorrect acceptances. We discuss the implications of guidance overhead for combinatorial synthesis across problem scales.
