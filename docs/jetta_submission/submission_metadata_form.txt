1. ARTICLE TITLE:
Guidance Overhead Across Circuit Scale in Learned Combinational Circuit Repair

2. RUNNING HEAD / SHORT TITLE:
Guidance Overhead in Circuit Repair

3. ABSTRACT (PLAIN TEXT FOR WEB PORTAL):
Machine learning guidance is frequently proposed to accelerate combinatorial search by prioritizing candidate modifications. We present a controlled empirical evaluation examining whether lightweight decision forests trained on compact combinational circuits (n in {4, 5} primary inputs, G in {10, 14} logic gates) can effectively guide single-edit repair on larger circuits (n <= 16, G <= 200) under strict wall-clock deadlines (0.1 s, 1.0 s, and 10.0 s). Evaluating a frozen protocol of 9,000 trials across 300 synthetic 2-LUT circuits, our results show that a direct Boolean CNF encoding solved via Glucose 4.2.1 in a CEGIS loop outperforms learned guidance under tight deadlines (<= 1.0 s). At 0.1 s on 250 larger circuits, direct CNF achieves an 81.6% repair rate compared to 20.8% for the learned ranker, as upfront guidance overhead (107.7–428.6 ms for G >= 100, scaling at 6.7–8.8 microseconds per candidate) exceeds the available budget. At 1.0 s, CNF maintains an advantage (81.6% vs. 74.4%, difference -7.2 pp, 95% CI [-10.4, -4.4]), with the 18-instance deficit confined to n=16 circuits. Extending the deadline to 10.0 s resolves 15 of these 18 instances, achieving cross-scale convergence (81.6% vs. 80.4%, repairing 201/204 solvable instances). Furthermore, learned ranking outperforms non-factored search heuristics (+8.4 pp) and unspecialized SMT (+17.6 pp) at 1.0 s. These findings emphasize the necessity of benchmarking learned guidance against factored symbolic baselines and accounting for operational proposal latencies across problem scales.
