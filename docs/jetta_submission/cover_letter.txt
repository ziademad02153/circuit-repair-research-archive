Background and Significance:
Automated logic debugging, fault diagnosis, and circuit repair are central challenges in Electronic Design Automation (EDA) and digital system reliability. A growing hypothesis in modern computer-aided design is that machine learning heuristics can accelerate combinatorial synthesis and repair loops by prioritizing candidate gate modifications. However, whether lightweight learned models maintain low enough inference latency to outpace modern, factored Boolean Satisfiability (SAT) solvers as circuit scale grows remains a critical, unresolved empirical question.

In this work, we present a controlled, large-scale empirical evaluation comparing lightweight histogram decision forests against non-factored search heuristics and modern Propositional Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF) SAT solving (Glucose 4.2.1 within a CEGIS loop) across a frozen protocol of 9,000 trials on 300 synthetic combinational circuits (scaling up to 16 primary inputs and 200 logic gates) under strict wall-clock deadlines (0.1 s, 1.0 s, and 10.0 s).

Key Contributions and Findings:
1. Empirical Guidance Overhead Analysis: Our results demonstrate that under tight sub-second deadlines (0.1 s), direct CNF substantially outperforms learned guidance (81.6% vs. 20.8% repair rate on 250 larger instances), consistent with upfront guidance overhead (107.7–428.6 ms, scaling near-linearly at 6.7–8.8 microseconds per candidate) consuming the entire operational budget before search begins.
2. Cross-Scale Convergence & Thresholds: Under extended deadlines (1.0 s and 10.0 s), learned guidance reaches complete parity with CNF across five of six scale strata (81.6% vs. 80.4% at 10.0 s, resolving 98.5% of solvable cases) while outperforming non-factored heuristics and legacy SMT.
3. Open Science and Reproducibility: To support full transparency, all 9,000 raw trial logs, frozen experimental protocols, trained model checkpoints, and automated standard-library audit scripts are preserved in a public research archive: https://github.com/ziademad02153/circuit-repair-research-archive.
